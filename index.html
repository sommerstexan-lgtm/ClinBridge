Flexible by Design

Use as little — or as much — as your doctor recommends

ClinBridge isn't one-size-fits-all. Every patient is different, and every doctor has different priorities. Start with the features your physician asks for, and add more only when it makes sense for your care.

💬
The right approach: Show your doctor what ClinBridge can track and ask them which features they'd find most useful for your care. Some patients use just the blood pressure log and nothing else — and that's exactly right for them. Others track eight different metrics every day. Both are correct. Your doctor decides what data matters for you.
🫀
Congestive Heart Failure (CHF)
CHF patients often need to monitor fluid retention closely. Ankle swelling, sudden weight gain, and blood pressure spikes can signal early trouble — giving your doctor early warning before a crisis develops.
Daily blood pressure (morning, evening)
Pulse tracking
Weight monitoring with alert threshold
Fluid intake log
Symptom log (swelling, shortness of breath)
Example reminders: "Morning BP + weight — 7 AM" & "Evening BP — 7 PM"
🩺
Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)
Kidneys and blood pressure are tightly linked. CKD patients often need strict fluid management — not too much, not too little — and a clear record of BP trends to help nephrologists fine-tune treatment.
Blood pressure log with trend analytics
Fluid intake with daily min/max targets
Medication timing and effectiveness
Symptom log (fatigue, swelling, headaches)
Fluid targets set per your nephrologist's specific orders — e.g. "between 48 and 64 oz per day"
🦴
Arthritis & Inflammatory Conditions
Many arthritis medications — including NSAIDs and steroids — can raise blood pressure over time. Tracking BP alongside your medication schedule helps your doctor see whether your treatment is affecting your cardiovascular health.
Blood pressure correlated with medication days
Symptom log (pain levels, flare days)
Medication timing reminders
Just three features — exactly what the rheumatologist needs, nothing extra.

Why I Built ClinBridge

Most people know more about Abraham Lincoln than their own blood pressure trend. ClinBridge exists to change that.

In November, I was sitting in my chair watching TV when I lost consciousness. When I woke up I didn't know what had happened — confused, too weak to stand or even crawl. I spent three days in the hospital being tested for everything. Eight years earlier I had survived a silent heart attack I didn't even know I was having. Now this. My cardiologist couldn't tell me with certainty what caused the episode.

When I got home I did what made sense to me — I built a spreadsheet, started researching, and began tracking everything I could. I wanted to understand my own body since medicine couldn't give me a clear answer.

Five months later, ClinBridge is the result of that work.

And the cause of that episode? My cardiologist told me: dehydration. I simply hadn't been drinking enough fluids. Something that simple nearly took me out — and no one caught it because no one had the data. When I got home I got busy building a spreadsheet to make sure it could never happen again. One thing led to another, and here we are.

That's exactly why this app exists. Free, private, built by a patient for patients — because better data saves lives, and it shouldn't cost you anything to have it.

Robert Sommers
Cardiac patient • Creator of ClinBridge

Prove It Yourself

Don't take our word for it. Here's how to verify ClinBridge never sends your data anywhere.

First — Get the best experience
✅ You're already running the installed version — you're all set for the full test below.

One tap installs it — no app store needed.

  • Tap the Share icon at the bottom of Safari (the box with an arrow)
  • Scroll down and tap "Add to Home Screen"
  • Tap Add — that's it

Then open ClinBridge from your home screen and come back to run the test.

For the strongest test, save ClinBridge to your home screen first. On Chrome, tap the menu (⋮) and choose "Add to Home screen". On other browsers, the tab-based test below still works — just don't close the tab.

1
Open ClinBridge from your home screen icon and enter a blood pressure reading or any data you'll recognize.
2
Enable airplane mode. You are now fully offline.
3
Close the app completely and reopen it from the home screen icon.
4
Your data is still there. The app still works. Every feature still functions.
✅ If it works in airplane mode with the app fully closed and reopened, your data was never sent anywhere. It lives only on your device.
Now try it with another health app. Enable airplane mode, close it, reopen it. Does it work? Does it even open? Most apps require a server connection because your data isn't really yours — it lives on their servers. That's the difference.
1
Open ClinBridge and enter a reading or some data. Keep this tab open — don't close it.
2
Enable airplane mode. You are now fully offline.
3
Switch back to the ClinBridge tab (don't reload). Navigate sections, add more entries, open charts.
✅ Everything still works while you're offline. That proves all data operations are purely local — nothing was ever sent to a server.
Note: If you reload the page in airplane mode, your browser will show a network error — that's expected. Your browser needs internet to fetch the app file from GitHub. That has nothing to do with your health data.
Now try it with another health app. Keep the tab open, go offline, and try using it. Most apps will either stop working or show errors — because their core functions depend on talking to a server. With ClinBridge, nothing changes.
